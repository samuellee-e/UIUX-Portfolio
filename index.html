Driven Well AI

Driver Ergonomics & Fatigue Management System

📋 Project Overview

🎯
Objective
Design AI-driven system to proactively monitor driver posture and fatigue, improving comfort, safety, and long-term health
👥
Target Users
Daily commuters, long-distance drivers, e-hailing and delivery drivers experiencing discomfort and fatigue
⚠️
Problem Space
Gap between ergonomic vehicle design and actual driver behavior leads to poor posture, discomfort, and increased accident risk

🔄 Design Process

Research: Conducted contextual inquiry in realistic driving scenarios with semi-structured interviews involving students, lecturers, and long-distance drivers to identify ergonomic pain points and fatigue patterns.

Ideation: Brainstormed AI-supported features including posture tracking, fatigue detection, and smart break recommendations across multiple dashboard concepts.

Wireframing: Produced three initial design concepts focusing on dashboard-based feedback.

Prototyping: Created paper prototypes for infotainment and instrument cluster screens, then developed digital mock-ups through iterative refinement.

Testing: Performed heuristic evaluations with peers and controlled usability testing with students, long-distance drivers, and ergonomics specialists across multiple iterations.

🎥 Project Walkthrough

🎯 Challenges & Solutions

Challenge: Alerts caused distraction while driving
Solution: Introduced steering-wheel button to dismiss alerts instead of touchscreen interaction, improving safety and efficiency.
Challenge: Users couldn't understand what AI was detecting
Solution: Added system-status messages and confirmation feedback like "Posture Fixed" for clarity.
Challenge: Fatigue detection felt abrupt and unclear
Solution: Implemented gradual fatigue indicators with icons and levels instead of single sudden alert.
Challenge: Posture feedback lacked actionable guidance
Solution: Added simple posture correction suggestions aligned with visual cues.

📊 Results

3 Usability Testing Rounds
High Clarity & Safety Rating
Validated Real-World Suitability

Final design achieved high clarity, safety, and interpretability. Users appreciated automatic operation with minimal setup. Evaluators validated system as suitable for real-world driving contexts.

As UX Researcher & Designer in this 6-member team, I participated in user research and interview analysis, contributed to task analysis and design ideation, was involved in paper prototyping and storyboard development, assisted in heuristic evaluation and usability testing sessions, contributed to design revisions based on user feedback, and supported documentation and final report preparation.

SunEvent

Campus Event Management Platform

📋 Project Overview

🎯
Objective
Create centralized, user-friendly mobile platform to reduce missed events, improve discoverability, and enhance registration efficiency
👥
Target Users
Sunway University students (ages 16-25) with varying technical proficiency, plus university staff and event organizers
⚠️
Problem Space
Fragmented event information across emails, social media, and messaging leads to missed events and cognitive overload

🔄 Design Process

Research: Conducted Google Form surveys with students and staff, identifying pain points like inbox overload and poor event visibility through refined questionnaires.

Ideation: Defined core UX problems around discoverability, navigation, and reminders. Created user personas and mapped user journeys focusing on event discovery.

Wireframing: Designed low-fidelity wireframes for Home, Event Discovery, Event Details, Registration, and Admin Dashboard screens with focus on layout clarity.

Prototyping: Developed high-fidelity prototypes in Figma with consistent color schemes, typography, and iconography for mobile and web versions.

Testing: Conducted usability testing with students and staff, evaluating task completion, clarity, and navigation flow for iterative refinement.

🎥 Project Walkthrough

🎯 Challenges & Solutions

Challenge: Poor Event Discoverability
Solution: Introduced searchable listings, category filters, highlighted upcoming/recommended events, and clearer event cards.
Challenge: Confusing Navigation Flow
Solution: Refined logic so users redirect to My Events after registration and access details directly from notifications and home cards.
Challenge: Unclear or Missing Information
Solution: Added organizer details, duration, capacity indicators, clickable locations, and clearer placeholder text in forms.
Challenge: Reminder & Notification Confusion
Solution: Separated reminders and notifications using segmented controls with customizable reminder settings.
Challenge: Visual and Layout Inconsistencies
Solution: Improved alignment, removed redundant admin elements, refined color palette for consistent layout.

📊 Results

76.5% Increased Engagement Potential
30+ Survey Responses Analyzed
100% Task Completion Rate

Fully interactive high-fidelity prototype demonstrated improved usability and clarity. All usability test participants successfully completed key tasks. Users found the app easy to navigate and intuitive with strong positive response toward centralized event information and reminder features.

👤 My Role

In this 6-member team project, I participated in UX research design and analysis, assisted in persona creation and user journey mapping, designed and refined wireframes and high-fidelity UI screens, conducted and documented usability testing, analyzed usability feedback and proposed UX-driven refinements, and contributed to UX rationale, reflection, and final documentation. My role focused primarily on UX logic, usability analysis, and iterative improvement.
